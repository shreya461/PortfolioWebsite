Overview

What the trip was, who I worked with, and the problem we set out to solve.

For six weeks in the summer of 2026 I lived and worked in Cape Town, South Africa as part of a study-abroad program that pairs student engineers with local organizations doing real work. My placement was with GRIT — Gender Rights in Tech, a Cape Town nonprofit that opens doors to technology careers for women and gender-diverse people.

GRIT runs on grants, donations, and a small, fiercely committed team. Their finances lived across spreadsheets and inboxes — accurate, but hard to see all at once. When a funder asked “where did this money go?” the answer took hours to assemble. Our brief was simple to say and hard to do well: give GRIT a single place to see their money — grants coming in, budgets spent, and reports they could hand to funders without dread.

Over the program we designed and built a financial-tracking web application in React, backed by a database, with dashboards and charts, grant and donor tracking, and budgets and reports. This page is the case study: the project, a walkthrough of what we shipped, an honest reflection, and a week-by-week ledger of what I learned.

02

The project

A financial-tracking web app for GRIT — built in React, backed by a database.

The problem

GRIT’s funding came from many sources with many rules. Tracking it in spreadsheets meant the full picture only existed in someone’s head. Reporting to funders was slow, and it was hard to answer basic questions: how much of this grant is left? Which budgets are overspent? What did we raise this quarter?

What we built

A single, secure web app where the team logs funds once and sees everything update — totals, trends, and per-grant balances — with reports they can export and send.

  • 01

    Dashboards & charts

    An at-a-glance view of funds in and out, balances, and trends over time — so the whole team reads the same numbers.

  • 02

    Grant & donor tracking

    Every grant and donor as a first-class record: amount, purpose, and how much remains — no more hunting across tabs.

  • 03

    Budgets & reports

    Set budgets, watch them against real spend, and export clean reports built for the questions funders actually ask.

03

Walkthrough

A guided tour of the app we shipped.

  1. Step 01

    Secure sign-in

    Finances are sensitive, so the app opens behind a login. Team members sign in to their own workspace — the data never lives in a shared spreadsheet link again.

  2. Step 02

    The dashboard

    Landing here, the team sees total funds, active grants, and spend trends rendered as charts. The numbers that used to take an afternoon to assemble are the first thing you see.

  3. Step 03

    Log funds once

    Income and expenses are entered once and categorized. Every dashboard, balance, and report updates from that single source of truth — no double entry, no drift between tabs.

  4. Step 04

    Track grants & donors

    Each funding source is its own record with a running balance. At a glance the team can see what a grant was for and exactly how much of it is left to spend.

  5. Step 05

    Budgets & reports

    Budgets sit next to real spend, so overspending shows up early. When a funder asks for an accounting, a clean report is a few clicks away instead of a lost afternoon.

04

Reflection

The honest version — what changed in how I think about building software.

I arrived thinking the hard part would be the code. It wasn’t.

The hard part was understanding a problem that wasn’t mine — sitting with GRIT’s team, hearing how they actually worked, and resisting the urge to build the app I imagined instead of the one they needed. The best hour I spent some weeks wasn’t writing React; it was watching someone reconcile a grant by hand and finally understanding why the “obvious” feature I’d sketched would have made their week harder, not easier.

Building for a nonprofit sharpened every engineering instinct. There was no room for a tool that only I could maintain, so I learned to write for the person who would inherit it. Constraints — a small team, limited time, real money on the line — turned out to be a gift. They forced clarity about what actually mattered: that the numbers were trustworthy, that the team could use it without me in the room, and that a funder’s question could be answered in minutes.

Cape Town taught the rest. Working across a different context, culture, and set of assumptions made me a more careful listener and a humbler builder. I came home with a finished app I’m proud of — and with a clearer sense that the point of the skill is what you do with it and who it’s for.

— Shreya, Cape Town, Summer 2026

The six-week ledger

We built a tool to track what GRIT gained each month. Here’s what I gained each week — entered like statement lines.

Wk Focus Lesson gained
01 Arrival & orientation Listen before you build. Week one was GRIT’s story, not my roadmap — the problem is never the one on the brief until you’ve heard it in their words.
02 Discovery & scoping Requirements are a conversation. Shadowing the team turned a vague “track our finances” into concrete, buildable features — and cut the ones nobody needed.
03 Architecture & data model Design the data first. Getting grants, budgets, and transactions right on paper made every React component afterward almost fall into place.
04 Dashboards & charts A chart is an argument. Turning rows of numbers into a view the team could read in seconds taught me that clarity is a feature, not a finish.
05 Grants, budgets & feedback Ship, then listen again. The first version I demoed was wrong in small, important ways — real feedback made the second version actually theirs.
06 Handoff & farewell Done means someone else can run it. Documentation and a clean handoff mattered as much as the code — a tool only helps if it outlives the builder.
